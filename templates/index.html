Framingham Heart Study Protocol & Preprocessing Framework

The Framingham Heart Study represents one of the most authoritative prospective cardiovascular epidemiological investigations in modern medicine. This system leverages 15 clinical indicators collected across 4,240 records to predict the binary target endpoint TenYearCHD.

5-Stage Leak-Free Preprocessing Pipeline (70:30 Stratified Ratio):
  • Missing Value Audit: Seven physiological biomarkers contained missing measurements: Fasting Glucose (9.15%), Education (2.48%), BPMeds (1.25%), Total Cholesterol (1.18%), Cigs/Day (0.68%), BMI (0.45%), and Heart Rate (0.02%).
  • Stratified 70:30 Split: 2,968 training cases (70%) and 1,272 testing cases (30%) strictly preserving identical 15.2% CHD positive class prevalence.
  • Leak-Free Median Imputation: SimpleImputer(strategy="median") fitted exclusively on training data and applied onto the test partition.
  • Z-Score Feature Standardization: Continuous variables transformed to zero mean (μ = 0) and unit variance (σ = 1) via StandardScaler fitted solely on the training partition.
  • Deep Latent Fusion (Hybrid): PyTorch ANN encoder generates 16-dimensional latent feature embeddings concatenated with the 15 standardized raw biomarkers into a 31-feature hybrid vector consumed by XGBoost.

Deep Learning Architecture Specifications:

• Artificial Neural Network (ANN): 4 dense layers [15 → 64 → 32 → 16 → 1] with Batch Normalization, ReLU activations, dropout regularization (0.25 / 0.15), and AdamW optimizer.
• Recurrent Networks (LSTM & GRU): 2-layer recurrent sequence models (hidden dimension = 32, dropout = 0.15) mapping sequential physiological dependencies into a dense classification head.

AHA / ACC Guideline Risk Rules:

Automated clinical warnings evaluate patient measurements against American Heart Association / American College of Cardiology standards: Stage 2 Hypertension (≥140/90 mmHg), Stage 1 Hypertension (≥130/80 mmHg), High Total Cholesterol (≥240 mg/dL), and Diabetic Fasting Blood Glucose (≥126 mg/dL).
